    About diving

    Diving is judged on takeoff, flight and entry from the springboard or the ten-metre platform. Synchronised diving pairs two athletes who must match each other exactly. China has dominated for decades, with Great Britain, the United States, Mexico and Australia also strong.

    What makes a good diving team name?

    Diving is a precision sport of springboard and platform dives, judged on form, difficulty and entry. The best diving team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Platform Divers, Springboarders or Pike Masters, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Platform Divers, Springboarders or Pike Masters, so the name clearly belongs to diving.
    • Say it out loud. A good diving team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a diving side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For diving that means Platform Divers, Springboarders or Pike Masters, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
