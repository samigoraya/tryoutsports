    About bodyboarding

    Bodyboarding is ridden prone or dropknee on a short foam board with fins, and is known for huge barrels and aerials on heavy waves. Riders represent clubs and nations at the ISA world championship. Brazil, Portugal, Australia, Hawaii, Chile and South Africa have the deepest talent.

    What makes a good bodyboarding team name?

    Bodyboarding is wave riding lying down on a foam board, with spins and barrel rides. The best bodyboarding team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Body Boarders, Drop Knee Riders or Barrel Cruisers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Body Boarders, Drop Knee Riders or Barrel Cruisers, so the name clearly belongs to bodyboarding.
    • Say it out loud. A good bodyboarding team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a bodyboarding side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For bodyboarding that means Body Boarders, Drop Knee Riders or Barrel Cruisers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
