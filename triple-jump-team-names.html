    About triple jump

    The triple jump is a hop, a step and a jump in one rhythm, which makes it one of the most technical events in athletics. Six attempts decide it. Cuba, Portugal, Venezuela, Burkina Faso, Jamaica and the United States are among the strongest nations.

    What makes a good triple jump team name?

    Triple jump chains a hop, a step and a jump into one explosive effort. The best triple jump team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Hop Step Jumpers, Phase Pushers or Bounders, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Hop Step Jumpers, Phase Pushers or Bounders, so the name clearly belongs to triple jump.
    • Say it out loud. A good triple jump team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a triple jump side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For triple jump that means Hop Step Jumpers, Phase Pushers or Bounders, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
