    About show jumping

    Show jumping asks horse and rider to clear a course of fences without knocking them down or exceeding the time. Nations Cup and Olympic team events put three or four combinations together under one flag. Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden, Great Britain and the United States are the strongest nations.

    • Team size1 rider and horse, with teams of 3 or 4
    • Biggest eventsOlympic Games, FEI Jumping World Championships, Nations Cup

    What makes a good show jumping team name?

    Show jumping tests horse and rider over a course of fences, with faults for knockdowns. The best show jumping team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Jumpers, Clear Rounds or Fence Flyers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Jumpers, Clear Rounds or Fence Flyers, so the name clearly belongs to show jumping.
    • Say it out loud. A good show jumping team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a show jumping side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For show jumping that means Jumpers, Clear Rounds or Fence Flyers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
