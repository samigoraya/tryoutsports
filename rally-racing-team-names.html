    About rally racing

    Rallying races against the clock on closed roads of gravel, tarmac, snow and mud, with a co-driver reading pace notes to the driver. Teams run two or three cars and score manufacturer points. Finland, France, Estonia, Belgium, Wales and Japan have produced many of the sport's stars.

    What makes a good rally racing team name?

    Rally racing sends drivers and co-drivers through timed stages on gravel, tarmac or snow. The best rally racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Co Drivers, Stage Hunters or Gravel Gladiators, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Co Drivers, Stage Hunters or Gravel Gladiators, so the name clearly belongs to rally racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good rally racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a rally racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For rally racing that means Co Drivers, Stage Hunters or Gravel Gladiators, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
