    About kickboxing

    Kickboxing allows punches and kicks over rounds of two or three minutes, in rulesets from point fighting to full contact. Gyms enter whole squads at amateur tournaments. The Netherlands, Thailand, Russia, Italy, Brazil and Japan have the strongest traditions.

    What makes a good kickboxing team name?

    Kickboxing combines punches and kicks, with rules that differ across K-1, full-contact and low-kick styles. The best kickboxing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Low Kickers, Roundhouse Kings or Shin Crushers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Low Kickers, Roundhouse Kings or Shin Crushers, so the name clearly belongs to kickboxing.
    • Say it out loud. A good kickboxing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a kickboxing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For kickboxing that means Low Kickers, Roundhouse Kings or Shin Crushers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
