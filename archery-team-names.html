    About archery

    Archery shoots at a target 70m away in Olympic recurve, with ten rings scored from one to ten. Team events put three archers together, and mixed teams pair a man and a woman. South Korea has dominated for decades, with the United States, India, the Netherlands, France and Turkey also strong.

    What makes a good archery team name?

    Archery is the sport of shooting arrows at targets, with recurve, compound and barebow styles. The best archery team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Archers, Bullseyes or Arrow Slingers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Archers, Bullseyes or Arrow Slingers, so the name clearly belongs to archery.
    • Say it out loud. A good archery team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a archery side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For archery that means Archers, Bullseyes or Arrow Slingers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
