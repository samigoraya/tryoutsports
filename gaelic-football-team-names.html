    About Gaelic football

    Gaelic football mixes kicking, hand passing and catching, with scores through the posts for a point or into the net for a goal. It is run by the Gaelic Athletic Association and played by amateur clubs tied to a parish or town, who then feed county teams. Club names here almost always honour a place or a local figure.

    • Team size15 a side, in Ireland's county and club system
    • Biggest eventsAll-Ireland Senior Football Championship

    What makes a good Gaelic football team name?

    Gaelic football is an Irish field game mixing kicking, hand passing and catching, played by two teams of fifteen. The best Gaelic football team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Point Scorers, Goal Getters or Hand Passers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Point Scorers, Goal Getters or Hand Passers, so the name clearly belongs to Gaelic football.
    • Say it out loud. A good Gaelic football team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a Gaelic football side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For Gaelic football that means Point Scorers, Goal Getters or Hand Passers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
