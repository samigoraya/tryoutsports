    About water polo

    Water polo is a physical pool sport where players tread water for four quarters while passing and shooting at a floating goal. Players cannot touch the bottom, so fitness matters as much as skill. Hungary, Serbia, Croatia, Italy, Spain, Greece and the United States are the strongest nations.

    What makes a good water polo team name?

    Water polo is a physical swimming game of treading water, passing and shooting at floating goals. The best water polo team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Eggbeaters, Drivers or Hole Sets, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Eggbeaters, Drivers or Hole Sets, so the name clearly belongs to water polo.
    • Say it out loud. A good water polo team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a water polo side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For water polo that means Eggbeaters, Drivers or Hole Sets, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
