    About darts

    Darts is thrown at a board from 2.37m, scoring down from 501 and finishing on a double. Pub and county leagues run on teams of four to eight, and the World Cup of Darts pairs two players per nation. England, the Netherlands, Wales, Scotland, Australia and Belgium are the strongest.

    What makes a good darts team name?

    Darts is a pub favourite and a pro sport, with checkouts, doubles and treble twenties. The best darts team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Dart Throwers, Bullseyes or Treble Twenties, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Dart Throwers, Bullseyes or Treble Twenties, so the name clearly belongs to darts.
    • Say it out loud. A good darts team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a darts side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For darts that means Dart Throwers, Bullseyes or Treble Twenties, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
