    About endurance racing

    Endurance racing runs for six, twelve or twenty-four hours, with two to four drivers sharing a car and the crew rebuilding it on the fly. Le Mans is the most famous race of all, and the team, not the driver, is the headline. Germany, France, Japan, Italy and the United States lead the manufacturers.

    • Team size2 to 4 drivers share each car
    • Biggest events24 Hours of Le Mans, FIA World Endurance Championship, 24 Hours of Daytona

    What makes a good endurance racing team name?

    Endurance racing, like Le Mans, runs for hours or a full day, with teams of drivers sharing the car. The best endurance racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Night Racers, Stint Drivers or Pit Stop Crew, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Night Racers, Stint Drivers or Pit Stop Crew, so the name clearly belongs to endurance racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good endurance racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a endurance racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For endurance racing that means Night Racers, Stint Drivers or Pit Stop Crew, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
