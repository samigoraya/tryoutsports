    About finswimming

    Finswimming races with a monofin or bi-fins, in the pool and in open water, and is the fastest way a human can move through water unaided by a motor. Relays put four swimmers under one club or national name. Russia, Hungary, Italy, China, Colombia and Greece are among the strongest nations.

    What makes a good finswimming team name?

    Finswimming uses fins or a monofin to reach high speeds on the surface or underwater. The best finswimming team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Fin Fliers, Monofin Masters or Dolphin Kickers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Fin Fliers, Monofin Masters or Dolphin Kickers, so the name clearly belongs to finswimming.
    • Say it out loud. A good finswimming team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a finswimming side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For finswimming that means Fin Fliers, Monofin Masters or Dolphin Kickers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
