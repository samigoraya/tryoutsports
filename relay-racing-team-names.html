    About relay racing

    Relay racing is athletics' true team event: four runners share the distance and pass a baton inside a 20m changeover zone. The 4x100m and 4x400m are the classics, and a dropped baton ends a medal hope instantly. This is the one track event where the team name goes on the result sheet.

    • Team size4 runners per team
    • Biggest eventsOlympic Games, World Athletics Relays
    • Sport groupAthletics: Track & Field

    What makes a good relay racing team name?

    Relay racing relies on four-person teams and precise baton exchanges. The best relay racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Baton Passers, Anchors or Exchange Zone, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Baton Passers, Anchors or Exchange Zone, so the name clearly belongs to relay racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good relay racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a relay racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For relay racing that means Baton Passers, Anchors or Exchange Zone, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
