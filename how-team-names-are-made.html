Method and editorial policy

How these team names are made

What goes into the generator, what we leave out, and how to check a name before you put it on a shirt.

By TRYOUT Sports, custom team uniform maker. Updated 28 Nov 2026.

What this tool is

This is a free team name generator for 198 sports. You pick a sport, a tone and who the team is for, and it shows 30 names. It has no sign-up. The generator runs in your browser, and what you type into it (a city, a short description) is not sent to us.

It is made by TRYOUT Sports, a custom sportswear and team uniform manufacturer. The tool is free to use, and some people who find a name here go on to ask us about a uniform. That is the only link between the two.

How the names are made

  • Hand-written names. Every sport has its own written names, 4,154 in total across 198 sports. These are shown first, because they use the sport's own language, like Net Gains for soccer.
  • Word lists. The generator combines words from lists of sport terms, describing words and animals, grouped into 8 tones (classic, fierce, funny, cute, royal, mythical, spooky, space). It only uses a word where it fits the sport.
  • Your own details. A city or school is added only if you type one. Without it, the name stands on its own.
  • Randomness. Each click draws a fresh set, so the same page gives different names every time. The sample lists printed on each page are fixed and are chosen from the hand-written names.

What we filter out

  • Real teams. 202 names of real professional teams are blocked, so the generator does not hand you a name that already belongs to a franchise.
  • Words that do not belong. Unsuitable animals and describing words are removed from the lists, and some sport words are limited to the sports they fit.
  • Rankings. The lists of well-known teams on sport pages show examples of how real clubs choose names. They are not rankings, and they may be out of date.

What it cannot do

Names are combinations of everyday words, so some will sound odd and some will sit close to a name that already exists. The tool does not check whether a name is taken, trademarked, or allowed by your league. Treat each result as an idea to shortlist, and read it out loud before you commit.

Before you commit to a name

  • Read your league's rules. Leagues and schools sometimes limit names, for example around offensive words or names too close to another team.
  • Search for it. Search the name with your town or sport, and look for another team already using it.
  • Check handles and domains. If you want a team page, see whether the social handle and web address are free.
  • Look at trademarks. If you plan to sell merchandise, search your country's trademark register first. This page is not legal advice.
  • Ask the group. Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.

Corrections and contact

If a name is wrong, offensive, or too close to a real team, tell us through the contact page with the page address and the name. We will review it and fix it in the next update. Sport and league names are used only to describe the sports, and TRYOUT Sports is not affiliated with any league or team.

Questions people ask

Are the names checked against real teams?

Yes, names of real professional teams are blocked from the generator. That does not make a name safe to trademark, so search for it before you use it on merchandise.

Does the generator save what I type?

No. The generator runs in your browser and the city or description you type is not sent to us.

Why are some names odd?

Names are built from everyday words, so some combinations land better than others. Read a name out loud and keep the ones you like.
