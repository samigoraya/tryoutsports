    About drag racing

    Drag racing runs two cars at a time down a straight quarter-mile or 1,000-foot strip, with Top Fuel cars finishing in under four seconds.     What makes a good drag racing team name?

    Drag racing is a straight-line sprint where dragsters launch in seconds over a quarter mile. The best drag racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Dragsters, Christmas Trees or Burnout Boys, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Dragsters, Christmas Trees or Burnout Boys, so the name clearly belongs to drag racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good drag racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a drag racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For drag racing that means Dragsters, Christmas Trees or Burnout Boys, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
