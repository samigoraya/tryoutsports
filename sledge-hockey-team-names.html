    About sledge hockey

    Para ice hockey, long known as sledge hockey, is played by athletes seated on sledges using two short sticks to pass, shoot and push. It is fast, full-contact and played to the same three-period format as ice hockey. Canada, the United States, China, South Korea and the Czech Republic are the leading nations.

    What makes a good sledge hockey team name?

    Sledge hockey, or para ice hockey, is played on double-bladed sleds with short sticks. The best sledge hockey team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Sledgers, Sled Skaters or Puck Pushers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Sledgers, Sled Skaters or Puck Pushers, so the name clearly belongs to sledge hockey.
    • Say it out loud. A good sledge hockey team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a sledge hockey side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For sledge hockey that means Sledgers, Sled Skaters or Puck Pushers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
