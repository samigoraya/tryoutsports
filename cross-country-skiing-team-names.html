    About Cross-Country skiing

    Cross-country skiing races in classic and skate technique over distances from a sprint to 50km, and the relay is one of the biggest events in the Nordic calendar. Clubs are at the centre of the sport across Scandinavia. Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, Switzerland and Germany lead the sport.

    What makes a good Cross-Country skiing team name?

    Cross-country skiing is a long-distance snow sport, using classic and skate techniques. The best Cross-Country skiing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Nordic Skiers, Klister Kings or Kick and Glide, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Nordic Skiers, Klister Kings or Kick and Glide, so the name clearly belongs to Cross-Country skiing.
    • Say it out loud. A good Cross-Country skiing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Alliance, Syndicate, Collective or Brigade. Try your town plus Crew for a Cross-Country skiing side.
    • A word from the sport itself. For Cross-Country skiing that means Nordic Skiers, Klister Kings or Kick and Glide, which tells everyone what you play.
