    About vaulting

    Equestrian vaulting is gymnastics on a cantering horse, performed solo, in pairs or in squads of up to six, with a lunger controlling the horse on a circle. Squad routines are among the most spectacular sights in equestrian sport. Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France and the United States dominate.

    • Team size1, 2 or 3 vaulters per horse, plus a lunger
    • Biggest eventsFEI World Equestrian Games, FEI Vaulting World Cup
    • Sport groupEquestrian Sports

    What makes a good vaulting team name?

    Vaulting is gymnastics on a moving horse, performed individually or in teams. The best vaulting team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Vaulters, Horse Gymnasts or Canter Crew, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Vaulters, Horse Gymnasts or Canter Crew, so the name clearly belongs to vaulting.
    • Say it out loud. A good vaulting team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • A word from the sport itself. For vaulting that means Vaulters, Horse Gymnasts or Canter Crew, which tells everyone what you play.
    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Alliance, Syndicate, Collective or Brigade. Try your town plus Crew for a vaulting side.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
