    About Cyclo-Cross

    Cyclo-cross races short laps of mud, sand, steps and barriers through the winter, with riders shouldering their bikes over obstacles. Trade teams and local clubs both run squads through a dense race calendar. Belgium and the Netherlands dominate, with strong scenes in France, Great Britain, the United States and the Czech Republic.

    What makes a good Cyclo-Cross team name?

    Cyclo-cross is a muddy autumn and winter cycling discipline, with barriers and run-ups. The best Cyclo-Cross team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Mud Riders, Barrier Hoppers or Run Up Crew, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Mud Riders, Barrier Hoppers or Run Up Crew, so the name clearly belongs to Cyclo-Cross.
    • Say it out loud. A good Cyclo-Cross team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a Cyclo-Cross side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For Cyclo-Cross that means Mud Riders, Barrier Hoppers or Run Up Crew, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
