    About alpine skiing

    Alpine skiing races downhill, super-G, giant slalom and slalom against the clock, with hundredths of a second deciding medals. The team parallel event sends four to six racers head to head for a nation. Austria, Switzerland, Italy, France, Norway and the United States are the strongest nations.

    What makes a good alpine skiing team name?

    Alpine skiing is downhill skiing on groomed slopes, from slalom to super-G. The best alpine skiing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Downhillers, Slalom Skiers or Gate Crashers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Downhillers, Slalom Skiers or Gate Crashers, so the name clearly belongs to alpine skiing.
    • Say it out loud. A good alpine skiing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a alpine skiing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For alpine skiing that means Downhillers, Slalom Skiers or Gate Crashers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
