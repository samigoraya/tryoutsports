    About race walking

    Race walking is judged as well as timed: one foot must stay in contact with the ground and the front leg must straighten, or judges can disqualify you. Distances run from 20km to 35km and 50km. Japan, China, Italy, Spain, Sweden and Ecuador are the strongest nations, and there is a dedicated team championship.

    What makes a good race walking team name?

    Race walking is a disciplined endurance event where one foot must always touch the ground. The best race walking team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Walkers, Heel Toe Heroes or Straight Knees, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Walkers, Heel Toe Heroes or Straight Knees, so the name clearly belongs to race walking.
    • Say it out loud. A good race walking team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a race walking side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For race walking that means Walkers, Heel Toe Heroes or Straight Knees, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
