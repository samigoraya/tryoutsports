    About mountain biking

    Cross-country mountain biking races laps of a technical off-road circuit, with the Olympic format lasting about 90 minutes. The team relay sends four riders from one nation out in turn. Switzerland, France, the Netherlands, Italy, Great Britain and the United States are the strongest nations.

    What makes a good mountain biking team name?

    Mountain biking is cross-country, trail or enduro riding on rough terrain. The best mountain biking team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Trail Blazers, Singletrack Crew or Rock Gardeners, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Trail Blazers, Singletrack Crew or Rock Gardeners, so the name clearly belongs to mountain biking.
    • Say it out loud. A good mountain biking team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a mountain biking side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For mountain biking that means Trail Blazers, Singletrack Crew or Rock Gardeners, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
