    About harness racing

    Harness racing has horses trot or pace while pulling a light two-wheeled cart called a sulky, with the driver seated behind. Stables run strings of horses and employ a full team of grooms and work riders. The United States, France, Sweden, Italy, Australia and New Zealand are the main nations.

    • Team sizeA horse, a driver and a stable team
    • Biggest eventsHambletonian, Prix d'Amerique, Elitlopp
    • Sport groupEquestrian Sports

    What makes a good harness racing team name?

    Harness racing pairs trotters or pacers with drivers in sulkies. The best harness racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Trotters, Pacers or Sulky Crew, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Trotters, Pacers or Sulky Crew, so the name clearly belongs to harness racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good harness racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a harness racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For harness racing that means Trotters, Pacers or Sulky Crew, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
