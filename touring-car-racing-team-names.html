    About touring car racing

    Touring cars are production-based racers that run in short, close, contact-heavy races with success ballast to keep the field tight. Manufacturer and private teams both score team championships. Great Britain, Germany, Sweden, Australia and Argentina have the biggest championships.

    • Team size1 or 2 drivers per car, with manufacturer teams
    • Biggest eventsWorld Touring Car Cup, British Touring Car Championship, DTM
    • Sport groupMotorsports

    What makes a good touring car racing team name?

    Touring car racing uses modified road cars in close, door-to-door races. The best touring car racing team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Touring Cars, Door To Door or Paint Swappers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Touring Cars, Door To Door or Paint Swappers, so the name clearly belongs to touring car racing.
    • Say it out loud. A good touring car racing team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Check that nobody in your league already uses the name before you order uniforms.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a touring car racing side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For touring car racing that means Touring Cars, Door To Door or Paint Swappers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
