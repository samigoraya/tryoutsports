    About BASE jumping

    BASE jumping leaps from buildings, antennas, spans and earth, using a single parachute and sometimes a wingsuit. Jumpers work in small, trusted crews who scout exits and check each other's gear. Norway, Switzerland, Italy, the United States and France have the best-known jump sites.

    What makes a good BASE jumping team name?

    BASE jumping means leaping with a parachute from fixed objects like buildings, antennas, spans and earth. The best BASE jumping team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Base Jumpers, Cliff Leapers or Wingsuit Crew, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Base Jumpers, Cliff Leapers or Wingsuit Crew, so the name clearly belongs to BASE jumping.
    • Say it out loud. A good BASE jumping team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a BASE jumping side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For BASE jumping that means Base Jumpers, Cliff Leapers or Wingsuit Crew, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
