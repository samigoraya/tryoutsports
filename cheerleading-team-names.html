    About cheerleading

    Cheerleading squads perform stunts, pyramids, tumbling and dance in routines of two and a half minutes, judged on difficulty, execution and synchronisation. It is one of the biggest true team sports for youth athletes, with all-star gyms competing nationally. The United States dominates, with Canada, Mexico, Japan and Finland also strong.

    What makes a good cheerleading team name?

    Cheerleading mixes stunts, tumbling and chants, from sideline spirit squads to competition teams. The best cheerleading team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Flyers, Bases or Spotters, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Flyers, Bases or Spotters, so the name clearly belongs to cheerleading.
    • Say it out loud. A good cheerleading team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Alliance, Syndicate, Collective or Brigade. Try your town plus Crew for a cheerleading side.
    • A word from the sport itself. For cheerleading that means Flyers, Bases or Spotters, which tells everyone what you play.
