    About horseshoes

    Horseshoe pitching throws shoes at a steel stake 40 feet away, scoring three for a ringer and one for a close shoe. It is a backyard and club game with a long tournament tradition in North America. Leagues run in singles and doubles, and club names are usually proudly local.

    What makes a good horseshoes team name?

    Horseshoes is a lawn and backyard game of pitching shoes around a stake. The best horseshoes team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Ringers, Pitchers or Stake Hitters, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Ringers, Pitchers or Stake Hitters, so the name clearly belongs to horseshoes.
    • Say it out loud. A good horseshoes team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • A word from the sport itself. For horseshoes that means Ringers, Pitchers or Stake Hitters, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
