    About Australian rules football

    Australian rules football is a free-flowing game of long kicks, high marks and handballs, played on a cricket-sized oval by eighteen players a side. A goal through the middle posts is worth six points, a behind one. It is the biggest spectator sport in Australia.

    • Team size18 a side on a huge oval

    What makes a good Australian rules football team name?

    Australian rules football is a free-flowing game on a huge oval with high marks, handballs and long kicks. The best Australian rules football team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Speckies, Ruckmen or Rovers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Speckies, Ruckmen or Rovers, so the name clearly belongs to Australian rules football.
    • Say it out loud. A good Australian rules football team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Make a shortlist of five and let the team vote.
    • Match the tone to the team: fierce for competitive squads, funny or cute for social and youth teams.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a Australian rules football side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For Australian rules football that means Speckies, Ruckmen or Rovers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
