    About table tennis

    Table tennis is played on a nine-foot table with light bats and heavy spin, to the best of five or seven games up to eleven points. Team events pair several singles matches with a doubles. China has dominated for decades, with Japan, South Korea, Germany and Sweden in pursuit.

    What makes a good table tennis team name?

    Table tennis, or ping pong, is a lightning-fast table game of spin, reflexes and short rallies. The best table tennis team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Loopers, Chop Blockers or Smashers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Loopers, Chop Blockers or Smashers, so the name clearly belongs to table tennis.
    • Say it out loud. A good table tennis team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.
    • Keep it to one to three words so it fits a uniform, a banner and a scoreboard.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a table tennis side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For table tennis that means Loopers, Chop Blockers or Smashers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
