    About high jump

    The high jump asks an athlete to clear a bar on a single foot takeoff, almost always with the Fosbury flop. Three failures at a height end the competition. Qatar, Italy, the United States, Ukraine and Australia have held recent titles, and jumpers score points for their club at league meets.

    What makes a good high jump team name?

    High jump launches athletes over a raised bar using the Fosbury Flop. The best high jump team names usually borrow from the sport itself, such as Flop Masters, Bar Clearers or Sky Hoppers, then add a place, a color or a strong describing word.

    • Use the sport's own words, like Flop Masters, Bar Clearers or Sky Hoppers, so the name clearly belongs to high jump.
    • Say it out loud. A good high jump team name is easy to chant and easy to read on a jersey.
    • Pick a mascot that is easy to draw, since it will end up on shirts, flags and social media.
    • Add your hometown or neighborhood to make the name yours.
    • One collective word works on its own: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers or Alliance, with your town in front.

    How real teams choose a name

    Look down the list above and the same patterns keep coming back. Plenty of famous teams are just one collective word, like Crew, Union, United, Dynamo or Rovers, put behind a city name. Others use a plural animal, a local trade, or a word taken straight from the sport.

    • One collective word. A single word for the whole group: Crew, Union, Dynamo, Rovers, Alliance, Athletic or Sporting. Columbus Crew and Philadelphia Union are built this way, and it works for any sport. Try your town plus Crew for a high jump side.
    • A plural animal or mascot. Easy to chant and easy to draw on a shirt: Wolves, Vipers, Falcons, Bulls.
    • A local trade or landmark. Steelers, Brewers, Miners, Mariners and Railmen all come from the work a town was known for.
    • A word from the sport itself. For high jump that means Flop Masters, Bar Clearers or Sky Hoppers, which tells everyone what you play.
    • A colour or kit. Reds, Blues, Greens and Blacks have named clubs for over a century, and they match your uniform without any thought.
